Five is a good age for gifts that ask something of a kid — not just entertain them, but give them a small thing to get better at. These are the ones that tend to survive past the first week without getting shoved in a cupboard.

## What we looked for

- **Open-ended play** over single-use toys — a block set outlasts a themed action figure.
- **A slight skill stretch** — riding a bike, following simple instructions, building something.
- **Parent sanity** — nothing that needs constant supervision or produces a mess that takes an hour to clean.

## Our picks

Building blocks remain the most reliable pick at this age — kids this age have the patience for multi-piece structures but still want the tactile, hands-on feel of wood or chunky plastic over anything screen-based. A first bicycle is the other big milestone gift; look for a 16-inch frame with training wheels that can come off later, so it's not a one-year purchase.

For something a little different, a beginner science kit — simple, safe experiments with an adult nearby for the first couple of runs — tends to get requested "again" more than most other novelty gifts.
